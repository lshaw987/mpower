One of the biggest challenges I see in leaders (and in myself) is that we don't spend our time on the right things. Doing your best work takes conscious thought and planning. It doesn't happen by accident.

So here's a question. When you reflect, do you really know how you plan? Do you think about the time and the energy it takes to do great work? What does your role really need you to be doing?

Setting up well means changing a few cadences and habits, so you deliver well and feel proactive rather than reactive. Most of us do a really crappy job of this and end up working in the wrong quadrant.....

My solution, and now my reflection habit, is a session with a massive whiteboard, Do Not Disturb on, and some growth time set aside. What comes out is a picture of how I need to spend a week - time on one axis, energy on the other. This is the exercise, step by step. It needs a blank page (or the template in the PDF), a pen, and an hour you don't let anyone interrupt.

## Step 1 - List your must-dos and plot them

Decide what work is critical to doing your role well. Think about what the job actually is - as the principal, the CEO, the person running the business side. What are the must-dos? Brainstorm everything that needs to happen in a typical week and get it all out of your head onto the page. Make assumptions. Generalise and smooth your week. Don't make excuses about why you can't have a "typical" week.

Then draw two intersecting axes. Across the bottom, time investment, low to high. Up the side, input energy, low to high. Set a scale that works for you. For me, "low" time was an hour or less in total across the week, and "high" was a full day a week. Energy is your call - what drains you, what fills you.

Take every must-do and place it where it belongs in the quadrant, with the total weekly hours written next to it. Don't try to do the maths yet. Just think about HOW you should be spending your time each week.

## Step 2 - Add the rest of your life

Work is only one part. Now use a different colour for each main part of your life - family, hobbies, volunteer work, the other business, time in your "opposite world" - and repeat the process on the same page. Overlaying it all onto one matrix is where the picture gets honest.

## Step 3 - Take the reality check

This is the powerful bit. Capturing everything floating around in your head highlights a few things fast. First and foremost, it gives you a reality check on where you're already over capacity with what you think you need to be doing. Most of us carry unrealistic expectations of ourselves.

When I did mine, the hours told me I was over capacity before the week had started. My goal became getting the total down to 35 hours a week (ideally 30, but I might have to get there in stages).

So go through every item. What can be automated? What can be delegated? What shouldn't be done at all? Be realistic about the capacity you have, and ruthless about protecting your time for the things that matter most.

## Step 4 - Add what's missing, then sit on it

Now add the important things that never made the list because they're never urgent - white space, learning, thinking time. If they're not on the page, they won't be in the week.

Then leave it for a few days. Come back and add, edit and think carefully about every single item. The first version is a brain dump. The second one is a plan.

## Step 5 - Time-block by energy

Now take it to your calendar. Time-blocking groups similar activities together (I already did that), but the shift here is scheduling things for when you'll have the right energy for them. Strategy work is definitely not best done on a Friday afternoon.

The outcome, when it works, is a realistic and manageable weekly framework. The right priorities are being met, and there's room in it for your own health and wellbeing, not just output.

## Three things I leave every room with

**Boundaries.** If I can't trust that your answer is no when it needs to be no, I can't trust your yes. It's meaningless.

**Done is better than perfect.** The map doesn't need to be right first time. It needs to exist.

**Capability is not capacity.** Being able to do something is a different question from having the hours and the energy to do it well. Most of us plan off the first and burn out on the second.

I'd love to know how you plan well. What did your quadrant tell you?
