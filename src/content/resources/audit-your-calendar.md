Your calendar tells the real story of what matters. Not your strategy doc. Not the plan on the wall. Your calendar.

If culture is "what we do around here", then your calendar is a living record of your priorities, your values and your leadership habits. And sometimes.... it needs an audit.

These are the six prompts I use. Open the last four weeks of your calendar beside you and answer honestly - a line or two under each is plenty. The PDF has the same six with room to write.

## 1. What's missing?

Where are the white spaces for thinking, strategy or recovery? Are the most important things getting squeezed out by the most urgent?

## 2. Where are your people?

If leadership is about relationships, how visible is that in your week? Regular check-ins, development conversations, time to walk the floor - can you actually see them?

## 3. Where's the impact?

How much of your time is Q2 work - important but not urgent? (That's the top-right box of the Eisenhower matrix, and the one most of us starve.) It's where growth and long-term value live.

## 4. What are you protecting?

Your energy, your focus, your wellbeing. What's non-negotiable in your week, and how well is it actually being protected? If it hasn't made it into the calendar, is it truly a priority?

## 5. What needs to go?

Which meetings are happening out of habit rather than purpose? What could be an email? What can you delegate or say no to?

## 6. Does this reflect the leader you want to be?

If someone looked at your calendar, would they see your values in action? Would they see space for wellbeing, reflection and real leadership?

## Then design it

Your calendar is more than a time-management tool. It's a culture-shaping force - for you first, and then for everyone who works around you. Audit it like it matters, because it does. Then design it with intention. Own your own space and put the time where it moves things.
